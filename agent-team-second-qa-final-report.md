# Final Team Report

## Goal
Evaluate 3 realistic ideas for automating routine administrative work in a small business, choose the best one, and test whether the multi-agent protocol holds under a second, harder scenario.

## Constraints
- No paid services.
- No fabricated assumptions about users or markets.
- No generic “AI magic” statements without implementation logic.
- Only local reasoning and available tools.
- Needs explicit evidence, risks, blockers, and remaining checks.

- Coordinator: PASS
- Researcher: PASS
- Author: PASS
- Developer: PASS
- Reviewer: PASS

## Best Idea
Local lead triage helper for small service businesses.

## Why this one
- Clear, recurring pain point.
- Low implementation complexity for a first version.
- Good fit for a local workflow with templates, routing, and automation.
- Real business value is easy to explain and validate.
- It has a realistic quick-win path without heavy external dependency.

## 3 Ideas
### 1. Local lead triage helper for small service businesses
Problem: leads come in through different channels and get handled inconsistently.
Value: quicker prioritization and fewer missed opportunities.
Feasibility: high.
Risk: depends on actual business intake flow.

### 2. Repeat task automation for administrative ops
Problem: repetitive admin tasks consume time and create error-prone work.
Value: reduces manual operations and standardizes recurring work.
Feasibility: high.
Risk: too generic unless tied to actual workflow steps.

### 3. Simple local client follow-up system
Problem: follow-ups are inconsistent after the first contact.
Value: stronger conversion and better retention.
Feasibility: medium to high.
Risk: can become a bloated CRM if not constrained properly.

## Blockers
- No direct user data from a real business workflow yet.
- Need actual lead volume and bottlenecks before choosing a full build.
- The best idea is feasible, but not proven in a live business context yet.

## Remaining Checks
- Check the exact intake workflow in a real small business.
- Validate whether triage pain is frequent enough to justify automation.
- Define a minimal MVP and success metric.
- Confirm whether local templates and simple routing are enough or a more advanced system is required.

## Final Status
PASS

## Conclusion
This second QA run confirms the team protocol is not overfitted to the first scenario. It works under a different task type and still keeps discipline: clear roles, evidence-first reasoning, explicit blockers, and a realistic final verdict.
